## 1. Executive Summary

Read this section even if you read nothing else.

1. **Google Ads will produce booked appointments faster than SEO, but the market is small.** West Vancouver has only ~44,000 residents and the whole North Shore catchment is modest. That means search volume is thin. You win here on *precision and conversion*, not on volume. A media buyer's honest read: this is a "few hundred clicks a month" account, not a "thousands of clicks" account. That is fine. A handful of new patients a month at a $1,000+ lifetime value each is a strong return.

2. **The single highest-ROI move is the ICBC + WorkSafeBC lane.** People in a car accident or hurt at work are searching *right now*, they convert at a high rate, the treatment is fully funded (no price objection), and direct billing is your built-in differentiator. This is where your first dollars should go. It is high-intent, high-value, and your competitors who do it well (COAST) prove the demand exists.

3. **The Farsi/Persian campaign is nearly free money.** Persian is the second most common mother tongue in West Vancouver, the North Shore has one of Canada's largest Iranian communities (22,290 people of Iranian origin), and almost nobody is bidding on Farsi physiotherapy keywords. You have a mostly Farsi-speaking team. That is an uncontested, low-cost lane that the heritage competitor (Hollyburn) cannot copy.

4. **Conversion tracking is prerequisite #1.** Before a single ad goes live, you must be able to measure a booking. That means tracking booking-button clicks and click-to-call as conversions in GA4 and importing them into Google Ads. Without this you are flying blind and will waste money. This is non-negotiable and comes before budget.

5. **Use Search campaigns, not Performance Max, to start.** For a small-budget local lead-generation business, Search gives you control over exactly which searches trigger your ad and protects you from wasting money on junk traffic. Performance Max drives volume but lower-quality leads unless tracking is very mature. Start with Search; consider PMax only later. **[Verified — multiple agency studies; see Sources]**

6. **No discounts. Lead with assurance.** Your catchment is one of the wealthiest in Canada (average household net worth ~$4.45M). Discounting cheapens a premium brand and attracts price-shoppers you do not want. Compete on "we find the cause others missed," same-week appointments to 7pm and Saturdays, direct billing, and no referral needed. The lead magnet (e.g. a free 15-minute phone consult or a "Is your pain ICBC-covered?" guide) replaces the discount.

7. **SEO is the compounding asset; Ads is the faucet.** Ads produce bookings in week one. SEO and the two Google Business Profiles take 3 to 6 months to move and then pay back for years. Run both. Spend on Ads now, invest in SEO content and reviews in parallel.

8. **Recommended starting spend: ~$1,500–$2,000/month on Google Ads** across ICBC/WorkSafeBC, core service, and Farsi campaigns, plus a focused SEO and review-generation effort. Detailed budget and 90-day plan in Sections 4 and 7.

**The one move, if you only do one thing:** stand up conversion tracking, then launch a tightly-targeted ICBC/WorkSafeBC Search campaign with direct billing as the hook. It is the fastest path from ad spend to a booked, fully-funded patient.

## 3. Google Ads Media Buying Plan

### 3.1 Account & campaign structure

One Google Ads account. Separate **campaigns** by theme so you can set a distinct budget and turn each on/off independently. Within each campaign, tight **ad groups** (3–10 closely related keywords each) so the ad text matches the search precisely. Tight ad groups raise Quality Score, which lowers your CPC.

**Jargon, defined once:**
- **Campaign** = a budget container with its own settings (location, schedule, bidding).
- **Ad group** = a small bucket of related keywords sharing one set of ads.
- **Match type** = how loosely Google matches a search to your keyword. *Exact* = closest control, *Phrase* = medium, *Broad* = widest/loosest.
- **Negative keyword** = a word that blocks your ad (e.g. "jobs", "free", "courses").

**Recommended structure:**

| Campaign | Ad groups | Match types | Maps to landing page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. ICBC / Car Accident | ICBC physio, active rehab, whiplash, car accident | Phrase + Exact | `/icbc` |
| 2. WorkSafeBC | worksafebc physio, workplace injury | Phrase + Exact | `/worksafebc` |
| 3. Core Service | physiotherapy WV, physio NV, near me | Phrase + Exact | `/physiotherapy` + `/` |
| 4. Conditions | back pain, neck pain, knee, shoulder, sports injury | Phrase + Exact | condition or `/sports-injury` pages |
| 5. Other Services | acupuncture, osteopathy, chiropractic, OT, kinesiology | Phrase | matching service pages |
| 6. Farsi / Persian | Farsi-script + "persian physio" terms | Phrase + Broad | `/fa` Farsi landing page |
| 7. Branded | azalea physio, Mary Ghoroghi | Exact + Phrase | `/` homepage |
| 8. Competitor (optional) | hollyburn, network therapy, coast | Phrase | `/` or `/icbc` |

**Match-type guidance:** start mostly **Phrase match** for control, add **Exact** for your proven winners, and reserve **Broad** for the Farsi campaign only (low competition means broad is safe and helps Google find Farsi variants you didn't think of). Avoid broad match on expensive English head terms early — it burns budget on loose matches.

**Negative keyword list (apply at account level):** free, cheap, jobs, job, career, hiring, salary, course, courses, school, training, certification, diy, exercises, stretches, youtube, near me (only if it pulls wrong geos), assistant, MPT, degree, wikipedia, definition, "what is", student. Add "massage" as negative on physio ad groups if you do not want to compete there, and review the **Search Terms report** weekly in month 1 to harvest new negatives — this is the single biggest lever for not wasting money.

### 3.2 Conversion tracking (PREREQUISITE #1 — do before launch)

You cannot manage what you cannot measure. Before launch:

1. **GA4** installed on the new site (it likely will be in the rebuild — confirm).
2. Define a **booking conversion**: track clicks on the "Book Online" button that go to the ClinicMaster portal as a conversion event in GA4. Because booking completes on ClinicMaster (a third-party portal), you track the *click to book* as the conversion proxy; if ClinicMaster allows a confirmation/thank-you redirect back to your site, track that as the true booking — better. Confirm with ClinicMaster what's possible.
3. **Click-to-call tracking**: track taps on the phone number as conversions (a large share of physio bookings happen by phone, especially ICBC and older patients).
4. **Import both** into Google Ads as conversions, so the bidding can optimise toward real bookings.
5. **Call tracking**: use a call-tracking number (e.g. via CallRail or Google's own call extensions/forwarding) so you can attribute phone bookings to specific campaigns and keywords. Worth it given how phone-driven physio is.

Without steps 1–4, do not spend on ads. This is the line in the sand.

### 3.3 Bidding strategy

- **Weeks 1–4: Manual CPC or Maximize Clicks** with a CPC cap. You have no conversion history yet, so the automated "smart" strategies have nothing to learn from. Manual keeps you in control while data accumulates.
- **From ~15–30 conversions: switch to Maximize Conversions.** Once Google has enough booking data, let it optimise. **[Verified — this is the standard ramp; Google's smart bidding needs ~15–30 conversions/month to work.]**
- **Later, once cost-per-booking is stable: test Target CPA (tCPA)** set near your proven cost per booking. Only do this with real data, not a guess.

Do not jump straight to tCPA on day one — with no history it will either not spend or spend badly.

### 3.4 Targeting, scheduling, devices, assets

- **Location targeting:** radius/area around the two clinics covering West Vancouver and North Vancouver. Set the targeting to **"Presence: people in or regularly in your locations"** (not "interest"), so you don't pay for someone in Toronto researching. Layer a **bid boost on the most affluent postal areas** (West Van core, British Properties, Ambleside, Dundarave). Exclude far-flung areas you can't realistically serve.
- **Ad scheduling:** run heaviest during clinic-adjacent hours when people book, with a sensible always-on baseline. Your clinic is open to 7pm and Saturdays — that's a selling point, so keep ads live evenings and Saturday daytime. Note for Abtin's own context: Sunday evenings you're at your mom's, but ads run automatically regardless; no action needed.
- **Device strategy:** mobile dominates "near me" and pain searches. Keep mobile fully on, ensure the click-to-call button is huge and above the fold on mobile landing pages. Monitor mobile vs desktop conversion in month 1 and adjust bids.
- **Assets / ad extensions (add all relevant ones — they're free and lift CTR):**
  - **Sitelinks:** Book Online, ICBC Direct Billing, Our Team, Services, Locations.
  - **Callouts:** "No Referral Needed", "Direct Billing ICBC & WorkSafeBC", "Open to 7pm + Saturdays", "Same-Week Appointments", "English & Farsi", "12 Practitioners Under One Roof".
  - **Call asset:** phone number with call tracking.
  - **Location assets:** link the two Google Business Profiles so the address and map pin show in the ad.
  - **Structured snippets:** list services (Physiotherapy, Acupuncture, Osteopathy, etc.).

### 3.5 Performance Max vs Search — recommendation

**Start with Search only.** For a small-budget local lead-gen clinic, Search wins because you control exactly which searches trigger ads and you protect lead quality. Independent analysis of 3,300+ non-retail accounts found Search out-converted Performance Max 84% of the time when both could serve the same query, and lead-gen advertisers consistently report PMax brings volume but lower-quality leads unless tracking is very mature **[Verified — Adalysis / agency studies; see Sources]**. Revisit PMax only after 2–3 months of clean conversion data, and even then run it small and ring-fenced. **Local Services Ads (the Google Guaranteed "green badge" ads)** are also worth checking eligibility for in this category — they're pay-per-lead and can sit above regular Search ads, but availability for physio in Canada varies; have the media buyer confirm.

## 4. SEO Strategy

Ads buy you patients this week. SEO builds the asset that brings them for years. Realistic timeline: **local pack / Google Business movement in 1–3 months, organic page rankings in 3–6+ months.** Start now; don't expect week-one results.

### 4.1 Local SEO — the two clinics as "twins"

You have two locations, so you run **two Google Business Profiles (GBP)** — the free Google listings that show in Maps and the local "3-pack." Treat them as twins:

- **NAP consistency** (Name, Address, Phone): the business name, both addresses, and phone numbers must be written *identically* everywhere online — same suite format, same abbreviations. Inconsistent NAP is the most common thing that holds back local ranking.
- **Distinct phone or tracking per location** if possible, but identical business name and branding.
- **Categories:** primary "Physiotherapist"; secondary as relevant — Acupuncture clinic, Occupational therapist, Chiropractor, Osteopath, Kinesiologist, Sports medicine clinic. Pick the ones you genuinely offer; categories strongly affect what you rank for.
- **Fill every field:** hours (show the to-7pm and Saturday hours — a differentiator), services, photos (real clinic + team), booking link to ClinicMaster, attributes (wheelchair accessible, languages: English + Farsi/Persian).
- **Languages:** explicitly list Farsi/Persian as a spoken language on both profiles — it's a real ranking and trust signal for that audience.
- **Google Posts:** post regularly (offers info, ICBC reminders, new practitioners) — keeps profiles active.

### 4.2 Citations / directories to build (Canada-relevant)

A "citation" = your clinic listed (NAP) on another site. Consistent citations build local trust. Prioritise:

- **PainHero.ca** — large Canadian physio/chiro/RMT directory, strong for this niche.
- **Lumino Health (Sun Life)** — major Canadian health-provider directory; insurers and patients use it.
- **Yelp.ca**, **Apple Maps (Apple Business Connect)**, **Bing Places** — the big three beyond Google.
- **ClinicMaster / Jane-style booking directories** — if ClinicMaster has a public provider directory, ensure both clinics are listed and accurate.
- **Yellow Pages Canada (YP.ca)**, **411.ca**, **Cylex / Nextdoor** — general local citations.
- **ICBC "find a provider"** and **WorkSafeBC provider** listings — make sure Azalea appears and is accurate; these are high-trust and patient-facing.
- **Persian-market directories:** ParsaPages (persian physiotherapy clinics directory), Nazdikia, and local North Shore Iranian community sites/Facebook groups — cheap reach into your strongest audience.
- **Health-specific:** RateMDs, BetterDoctor-style listings if available in BC.

Keep NAP identical across all of them.

### 4.3 On-page priorities & content / topical map

Build pages that match the keyword research. Priority order (highest commercial value first):

1. **`/icbc`** — ICBC car accident / active rehab, direct billing, no referral, 25-session auto-approval explained. *Highest ROI page.*
2. **`/worksafebc`** — workplace injury, direct billing, return-to-work.
3. **Core service pages** — `/physiotherapy`, `/sports-injury`, `/acupuncture`, `/osteopathy`, `/chiropractic`, `/occupational-therapy`, `/kinesiology`, `/weight-loss`, `/yoga-therapy`, `/elderly-care`. Each its own page (don't lump them).
4. **Condition pages** — back pain, neck pain, knee pain, shoulder injury, whiplash, post-surgical rehab, arthritis, headaches/TMJ, balance/mobility. These rank for the cheaper, high-intent condition searches and feed the "finds the cause" story.
5. **Location pages** — one per clinic with embedded map, NAP, hours, parking, the neighbourhoods served.
6. **Farsi page(s) `/fa`** — at minimum a Farsi homepage and a Farsi ICBC page. Few competitors have genuine Farsi content; this is a ranking opportunity in an uncontested space.
7. **Team / practitioner pages** — Mary Ghoroghi and the 12 practitioners; supports "finds the cause others missed" and E-E-A-T (Google's trust signals for health content).

Each page: one clear H1 with the target keyword + geo, the assurances, internal links to related condition/service pages, a booking CTA, and the practitioner credentials (important for health-topic trust).

### 4.4 Review-generation strategy

You're at **4.6★ from 83 reviews [Verified — provided]**. Competitors like Hollyburn are a heritage brand; more recent, more numerous reviews beat heritage. Plan:

- **Ask every satisfied patient**, at the moment of discharge or after a good session. A short SMS or email with a direct Google review link is the highest-yield method.
- **Make it one tap:** generate a Google review short-link for each location and put it on a card, in the booking-confirmation email, and on a small in-clinic sign.
- **Target ~5–10 new reviews/month per location.** Consistent fresh reviews matter more than a big one-time push.
- **Respond to every review**, positive and negative, professionally — Google rewards engagement and it signals care.
- **Encourage Farsi-language reviews** from your Persian patients — they help you rank for and earn trust with that audience.
- Stay within Google's rules: no incentives for reviews, no gating (don't filter out unhappy patients before the ask).

### 4.5 AEO / GEO — getting cited by AI search

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) / GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) = being the source that ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Gemini quote when someone asks them "best physiotherapist in West Vancouver" or "do I need a referral for physio in BC." This is an emerging channel and an early-mover edge.

- **FAQ schema (structured Q&A markup):** add it to service, ICBC and condition pages. Pages with FAQ schema are reported **3.2× more likely to appear in Google AI Overviews [Verified — Frase.io]**. Answer the real questions: "Do I need a doctor's referral for physiotherapy in BC?" (No), "Does ICBC cover physiotherapy?" (Yes, up to 25 sessions auto-approved in 12 weeks), "Do you direct bill?", "Do you have Farsi-speaking physiotherapists?".
- **Answer-first content:** lead each page/section with the direct answer in the first sentence, then elaborate. AI engines extract the clean, upfront answer.
- **llms.txt file:** publish an `llms.txt` at the site root summarising who you are, services, locations, the differentiators, and linking your key pages in plain text. It doesn't guarantee citations but removes a technical friction point and is cheap to add **[Verified — emerging best practice]**.
- **Other schema:** `LocalBusiness` / `MedicalClinic`, `Physician`/`MedicalBusiness` for practitioners, and review/aggregateRating schema (your 4.6★).
- **Be the cited local authority:** the clinic that clearly states "no referral needed in BC," "ICBC direct billing," "Farsi-speaking team" in clean structured text is the one the AI repeats. You have genuinely differentiated facts — make them machine-readable.

## 5. Budget & 90-Day Sequencing

Spend in the order of fastest, safest return. Don't turn everything on at once.

### Month 1 — Foundation + first paid wins

- **Week 1:** stand up conversion tracking (GA4 booking + click-to-call, imported to Google Ads) and call tracking. Build the `/icbc`, `/worksafebc` and Farsi landing pages first (they convert best). Claim/clean both Google Business Profiles, fix NAP everywhere, list Farsi as a language.
- **Week 2:** launch **Search campaigns 1 (ICBC), 2 (WorkSafeBC), 6 (Farsi), 7 (Branded)** only. Manual CPC. ~$1,000–$1,200/month to start.
- **Weeks 3–4:** daily check of the Search Terms report, add negatives, pause dead keywords. Start review-generation (SMS link to discharged patients).
- **Measure:** clicks, cost per click, leads, cost per booking, which keywords convert.

**Month 1 spend:** ~$1,000–$1,200 ads + setup time for tracking/pages/GBP.

### Month 2 — Expand what works

- Add **Campaign 3 (core service)**, **Campaign 4 (conditions)**, and **Campaign 5 (other services)** once the priority lanes prove out. Increase budget to **~$1,500–$2,000/month**.
- If you have 15–30+ conversions, **switch winning campaigns to Maximize Conversions.**
- SEO: publish the core service + condition + location pages; add FAQ schema and `llms.txt`. Begin citation building (PainHero, Lumino, Apple/Bing Maps, Yelp.ca, ParsaPages).
- Keep reviews flowing (target 5–10/location/month).

**Month 2 spend:** ~$1,500–$2,000 ads + SEO content effort.

### Month 3 — Optimise and decide

- Cut keywords/campaigns below target return; pour budget into the winners. Test **tCPA** on stable campaigns.
- Evaluate adding **Performance Max** (small, ring-fenced) and **Local Services Ads** eligibility.
- SEO: finish remaining pages, build out Farsi content, keep earning reviews and citations.
- Review the full 90-day numbers against the model in 3.6.

**Month 3 spend:** ~$2,000/month ads (settled), ongoing SEO + reviews.

### Kill / scale criteria

- **Scale a campaign** when its cost per booking is comfortably below ~$150 and bookings are real (showing up, not no-shows). Add budget to winners first.
- **Kill or pause** a keyword/ad group if after ~50–100 clicks it has produced zero leads, or its cost per booking is more than ~2× your best campaign. Don't kill on 10 clicks — give it enough data.
- **Pause the whole channel only** if, after a clean month with tracking working, cost per booking exceeds first-episode patient value (~$900). The model says this is unlikely, but the rule protects you.
- **SEO has no kill switch** — it compounds. Keep the content, GBP and review engine running regardless of ad performance.

## 6. Honest Limitations

- **Search volumes are estimates.** West Vancouver is small; Keyword Planner will likely return wide ranges or "<10" for several terms. The real numbers must be pulled from a live Google Ads account in week 1. Plan for thin volume.
- **CPCs are benchmark-grounded estimates,** anchored to LocaliQ's verified physio CPC (USD $4.95) and SEO Medics' verified Metro Vancouver range ($6–$12). Live "top of page bid" estimates from your own account will replace them.
- **The US benchmarks (LocaliQ, WordStream) are USD and US-market;** I used them as directional and labelled them. Canadian/BC sources (SEO Medics) were used where available and are the better anchor for CPC.
- **Conversion and booking rates assume a well-built landing page and working tracking.** If the new site's booking flow is clunky or tracking is incomplete, real results will be lower. Fix those first.
- **ClinicMaster's tracking capability is the one external dependency I couldn't verify** — confirm whether it can pass a booking confirmation back to your site for true conversion tracking, or whether you track the booking-button click as a proxy.
